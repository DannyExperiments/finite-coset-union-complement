\title{A Rank Method for Complements of Finite Coset Unions}
\date{2 August 2026}
\subjclass[2020]{20D60, 05B40, 15A03}
\keywords{finite group, subgroup coset, coset union, Hadamard product, matrix rank, exact-support rank}

\begin{document}
\begin{abstract}
We prove an affirmative answer to Kourovka Notebook Problem 21.115: if
$C_1,\ldots,C_n$ are arbitrary independently left or right cosets, possibly of
different nonnormal subgroups of a finite group $G$, and
$G\setminus\bigcup_i C_i$ is nonempty, then it has size at least $|G|/2^n$.
The proof constructs rank-two matrices whose Hadamard product has exactly the
complement Cayley support and compares its rank with a sparse-transversal lower
bound.  The same matrix yields left and right translate covers with at most
$2^n$ translates and shows that equality forces the uncovered set to be a
subgroup coset of index $2^n$.  For compatible blocks of $b_j$ left or right
cosets of a common subgroup, a univariate detector of rank at most $b_j+1$
gives the sharper denominator $\prod_j(b_j+1)$, with corresponding
translate-cover and equality statements.  We record sharp examples and several
compact consequences.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and statement of the problem}

Problem 21.115 first appeared in the official version 39 of the 21st
\emph{Kourovka Notebook}, dated 8 January 2026, on printed page 178
\cite[p.~178]{Kourovka21v39}.  It is attributed there to B.~Sambale and asks:

\begin{quote}
Let $C_1,\ldots,C_n$ be (left or right) cosets of a finite group $G$ such that
$U=C_1\cup\cdots\cup C_n$ is not $G$.  Is it always true that
$|G\setminus U|\ge |G|/2^n$?
\end{quote}

The wording permits the orientation to be chosen independently for each coset;
the underlying subgroups may differ and need not be normal.  In the current
checked version 45, dated 3 July 2026, the problem appears on printed page 181
with unchanged wording and no solution annotation \cite[p.~181]{Kourovka21v45}.
The same bound had been formulated conjecturally by Sambale and
T\u{a}rn\u{a}uceanu, who proved a general $|G|/(2n!)$ lower bound and the desired
estimate in several special cases \cite{SambaleTarnauceanu2022}.

Our main result is the exact affirmative answer.

\begin{theorem}[Mixed-coset complement bound]\label{thm:main}
Let $n\ge 1$, let $G$ be a finite group, and let $C_1,\ldots,C_n$ be sets such
that each $C_i$ is a left or right coset of a subgroup of $G$.  The orientations,
subgroups, and repetitions may be arbitrary.  If
\[
  A:=G\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^n C_i\ne\varnothing,
\]
then
\[
  |A|\ge \frac{|G|}{2^n}.
\]
Equivalently, $|A|\ge\lceil |G|/2^n\rceil$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is an exact rank sandwich.  One constructs a matrix $M$ with
$M(x,y)\ne0$ precisely when $x^{-1}y\in A$, proves $\rank M\le2^n$, and uses
the support of an actual row basis to prove $\rank M\ge |G|/|A|$.  This also
produces a nonsingular submatrix whose nonzero entries are all supported on the
complement relation.

The same argument gives two structural strengthenings.  First, at most $2^n$
left translates of $A$ cover $G$, and the same is true on the right.  Second,
equality in \cref{thm:main} forces $A$ to be a subgroup coset of index $2^n$.
When several forbidden cosets share one subgroup and one orientation, their
individual rank-two factors can be compressed into a single rank-$(b+1)$
block.  A targeted search through 2 August 2026 did not locate this exact
mixed-orientation compatible-block construction or its product-denominator
consequences; it is therefore presented only as an apparently new application,
with moderate confidence and no claim of historical priority.

Several ingredients and later consequences have established antecedents.
Hadamard-rank submultiplicativity is standard
\cite[Observation~7, p.~587]{Johnson1978} and \cite[Proposition~2, p.~27]{ArvindJoglekarSrinivasan2009}; the sparse
support lemmas below are convenient elementary formulations of basis and minor
facts, with close support--dimension analogues in permutation modules
\cite{GoldsteinGuralnickIsaacs2005}.  The binary subgroup-chain example is a
special case of Sun's exact-cover construction \cite[Example~1.2]{Sun2001}; see also \cite{Sun2006}.
The exact-support parameter used in the appendix is the Cayley specialization
of established zero-pattern and nondeterministic-rank notions
\cite{HershkowitzSchneider1993,DeWolf2000}.  The finite-index and profinite
statements are routine quotient transfers.  None of these observations is a
priority certificate for the main theorem or the compatible-block refinement.

Two distinct audited Lean 4/Mathlib certificates verify the headline
mixed-left/right finite-group complement inequality: an independently written
Lean 4.30 proof and an Aristotle reconstruction with an audited one-line
compatibility port to Lean 4.30.  Neither certificate formalizes sharpness,
translate-cover conclusions, equality rigidity, compatible grouped blocks,
exact-support rank consequences, prescribed-image applications, or the
finite-index and profinite extensions.

\subsection*{The 2022 special cases}
The following facts were already established in
\cite{SambaleTarnauceanu2022}.  Proposition~2 gives the general estimate
$|G\setminus\bigcup_iC_i|\ge |G|/(2n!)$.  Proposition~3 proves the target
$2^{-n}$ estimate for unions of subgroups when $n\le5$, and Lemma~4 reduces the
nilpotent case to finite $p$-groups.  Theorem~6 gives an optimal formula for
elementary abelian $p$-groups: if $n=a+b(p-1)$ with $0\le a\le p-2$, then the
uncovered proportion is at least $(p-a)/p^{b+1}$.  The paper also obtains the
target bound for finite $p$-groups when $n\le2p-2$ and for cyclic groups.  The
argument below is different and treats arbitrary finite groups and arbitrary
mixed left/right subgroup cosets.

\section{Two elementary rank lemmas}

All ranks are ordinary matrix ranks over the stated field.  If matrices have
the same shape, $B\Had D$ denotes their entrywise, or Hadamard, product.

\begin{lemma}[Hadamard-rank inequality]\label{lem:hadamard-rank}
Let $s\ge1$, and let $B_1,\ldots,B_s$ be matrices of the same finite shape over a field $\F$.
Then
\[
  \rank(B_1\Had\cdots\Had B_s)\le\prod_{i=1}^s\rank B_i.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Write $B_i=\sum_{j=1}^{r_i}u_{ij}v_{ij}^{\mathsf T}$, where
$r_i=\rank B_i$.  Distributing the entrywise product expresses
$B_1\Had\cdots\Had B_s$ as a sum of $\prod_i r_i$ outer products, since
\[
 (u_1v_1^{\mathsf T})\Had\cdots\Had(u_sv_s^{\mathsf T})
 =(u_1\Had\cdots\Had u_s)(v_1\Had\cdots\Had v_s)^{\mathsf T}.
\]
Each summand has rank at most one.  This is the standard Hadamard-rank bound;
see also \cite[Observation~7, p.~587]{Johnson1978} and
\cite[Proposition~2, p.~27]{ArvindJoglekarSrinivasan2009}.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[Sparse transversal and supported minor]\label{lem:sparse}
Let $B$ be an $N\times N$ matrix over a field, where $N>0$.  Suppose every row
has at most $m$ nonzero entries and there is a permutation $\pi$ of the column
set such that $B(x,\pi(x))\ne0$ for every row $x$.  Then, writing
$r=\rank B$,
\[
  N\le mr.
\]
Moreover, $r$ actual row supports cover all columns, $r$ actual column supports
cover all rows, and there are actual row and column index sets $X,Y$ with
$|X|=|Y|=r$ such that $B[X,Y]$ is nonsingular.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Choose $r$ actual rows forming a basis of the row space.  If a column vanished
on those rows, it would vanish on every row.  This is impossible because the
transversal supplies a nonzero entry in every column.  Thus the basis-row
supports cover all $N$ columns, and $N\le mr$.  Applying the same argument to
columns gives the transposed support-cover assertion.  Finally, the $r\times N$
matrix formed by the basis rows has row rank $r$, so it has $r$ pivot columns;
the corresponding $r\times r$ submatrix is nonsingular.
\end{proof}

In particular, a square matrix with nonzero diagonal and at most $m$ nonzeros
per row has rank at least $N/m$.  These statements are elementary basis and
rank--minor facts; they are recorded in this form only because every part is
used below.

\section{The rank-two detector and the proof}

\subsection{Orientation and normalization}
For every subgroup $H\le G$ and $a\in G$,
\[
  Ha=a(a^{-1}Ha).
\]
Indeed, both sets consist of the elements $ha$ with $h\in H$.  Hence every
right coset is a left coset of a conjugate subgroup, and this conversion may be
made independently for each $C_i$.  We may write
$C_i=a_iH_i$ without assuming that any $H_i$ is normal.

Choose $g_0\in A$ and left-translate the entire configuration by $g_0^{-1}$.
Each $a_iH_i$ remains a left coset, cardinalities are unchanged, and the
translated complement contains the identity.  After renaming, we assume
\[
  e\in A,\qquad C_i=a_iH_i,\qquad a_i\notin H_i.
\]
The last condition is equivalent to $e\notin a_iH_i$.

\subsection{One forbidden coset}
Regard $G/H_i$ only as the finite set of left cosets and choose an injection
$\lambda_i:G/H_i\to\Q$.  Define a $G\times G$ matrix by
\[
  M_i(x,y)=\lambda_i(yH_i)-\lambda_i(xa_iH_i).
\]
Writing $\alpha_i(y)=\lambda_i(yH_i)$ and
$\beta_i(x)=\lambda_i(xa_iH_i)$ shows that
$M_i(x,y)=\alpha_i(y)-\beta_i(x)$, the difference of two outer products;
therefore $\rank M_i\le2$.  Injectivity gives the exact zero pattern
\[
\begin{aligned}
 M_i(x,y)=0
 &\Longleftrightarrow yH_i=xa_iH_i\\
 &\Longleftrightarrow x^{-1}y\in a_iH_i=C_i.
\end{aligned}
\]
No quotient-group operation is used.  In particular, $M_i(x,x)\ne0$ because
$e\notin C_i$.

\subsection{The complement matrix}
Set
\[
  M=M_1\Had\cdots\Had M_n.
\]
Since $\Q$ has no zero divisors,
\[
  M(x,y)\ne0
  \Longleftrightarrow x^{-1}y\notin C_i\ \text{for every }i
  \Longleftrightarrow x^{-1}y\in A.                 \tag{3.1}\label{eq:support}
\]
Thus row $x$ has support $xA$ and exactly $|A|$ nonzero entries, while every
diagonal entry is nonzero.  By Lemma~\ref{lem:hadamard-rank},
$\rank M\le2^n$.  Equivalently, direct expansion gives
\[
 M(x,y)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}(-1)^{|S|}
 \left(\prod_{i\in S}\beta_i(x)\right)
 \left(\prod_{i\notin S}\alpha_i(y)\right),       \tag{3.2}\label{eq:expansion}
\]
a sum of $2^n$ outer products.

Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse} with the diagonal transversal.  With $N=|G|$ and
$m=|A|$,
\[
  \frac{|G|}{|A|}\le\rank M\le2^n,
\]
which proves \cref{thm:main} in the normalized configuration.  Undoing the
left translation preserves $|A|$ and completes the proof.

The same lemma supplies an explicit internal certificate: there are
$X,Y\subseteq G$ with $|X|=|Y|=\rank M$ such that $M[X,Y]$ is nonsingular.
Every nonzero entry of this minor satisfies $x^{-1}y\in A$ by
\cref{eq:support}.  The minor is not asserted to be principal or to equal
$M[A,A]$.

\section{Translate covers and equality}

For a nonempty subset $A\subseteq G$, define
\[
 \tau_L(A)=\min\{|T|:T\subseteq G,\ TA=G\},\qquad
 \tau_R(A)=\min\{|T|:T\subseteq G,\ AT=G\}.
\]

\begin{theorem}[Two-sided translate covers]\label{thm:translate}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:main},
\[
  \tau_L(A)\le2^n,\qquad \tau_R(A)\le2^n.
\]
More precisely, in the normalized construction both numbers are at most
$\rank M$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Let $x_1,\ldots,x_r$ index actual basis rows of $M$, where $r=\rank M$.
Their supports cover all columns by Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse}; by
\cref{eq:support}, these supports are $x_1A,\ldots,x_rA$.  Hence
$G=\bigcup_{j=1}^r x_jA$.

For fixed $y$, the support of column $y$ is
\[
  \{x:x^{-1}y\in A\}=yA^{-1}.
\]
Actual basis columns therefore give $G=\bigcup_j y_jA^{-1}$; taking inverses
gives $G=\bigcup_j Ay_j^{-1}$.  Since $r\le2^n$, the normalized bounds follow.
If $A'=g_0^{-1}A$ is the normalized complement, then
$TA'=G$ implies $(Tg_0^{-1})A=G$, while $A'T'=G$ implies $AT'=G$.
\end{proof}

As a counting certificate, choose one representation $g=t(g)a(g)$ from a left
cover $TA=G$.  Then $g\mapsto(t(g),a(g))$ injects $G$ into $T\times A$.

\begin{theorem}[Equality rigidity]\label{thm:equality}
Under the hypotheses of \cref{thm:main}, if $|A|=|G|/2^n$, then $A$ is a
subgroup coset of index $2^n$.  After normalization by a point of $A$, the
complement itself is a subgroup.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Work in the normalized setting $e\in A$ and put $m=|A|$, $N=|G|$.  Equality
forces
\[
  \rank M=2^n=N/m.
\]
Any basis of actual rows has $N/m$ supports, each of size $m$, covering $G$.
Their total size is $N$, so they are pairwise disjoint.

Suppose rows indexed by $x,y$ have overlapping supports.  If they were
independent, then, because the actual rows span the row space, they could be
extended to a basis of actual rows, contradicting the preceding disjointness.
The two rows are therefore dependent; neither is zero, so they are proportional
and have equal supports.  Consequently
\[
  xA\cap yA\ne\varnothing\quad\Longrightarrow\quad xA=yA. \tag{4.1}\label{eq:disjoint-equal}
\]
Let $H=\{g\in G:gA=A\}$.  If $a\in A$, then $a\in A\cap aA$ because
$e\in A$, and \cref{eq:disjoint-equal} gives $aA=A$; hence $A\subseteq H$.
Conversely, $h\in H$ implies $h=he\in hA=A$, so $H\subseteq A$.  Thus
$A=H$ is a subgroup.  Undoing normalization makes the original complement a
left coset of $H$, and $[G:H]=|G|/|A|=2^n$.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:family-open}
\Cref{thm:equality} classifies the uncovered set, not the covering family.
Classification of all families of $n$ left or right subgroup cosets whose union
is the complement of a subgroup coset of index $2^n$ remains open.
\end{remark}

\section{Compatible blocks}

\begin{definition}\label{def:block}
A compatible block of size $b\ge1$ is either $b$ left cosets
$a_1H,\ldots,a_bH$ of one subgroup $H$, or $b$ right cosets
$Ha_1,\ldots,Ha_b$ of one subgroup $H$.  A compatible block partition is a
partition of the given coset family into such blocks.
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Rank-$(b+1)$ block detector]\label{lem:block}
Every compatible block of size $b$ admits a rational matrix of rank at most
$b+1$ whose zeros are exactly the pairs $(x,y)$ for which $x^{-1}y$ lies in
one of the cosets in that block.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For a left block $a_1H,\ldots,a_bH$, choose an injection
$\lambda:G/H\to\Q$ and define
\[
  L_H(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^b
  \bigl(\lambda(yH)-\lambda(xa_jH)\bigr).
\]
The $j$th factor vanishes exactly when $x^{-1}y\in a_jH$.  Expanding as a
polynomial of degree $b$ in the single column variable $t=\lambda(yH)$ gives
$L_H(x,y)=\sum_{r=0}^b u_r(x)t^r$, a sum of $b+1$ outer products.  Hence
$\rank L_H\le b+1$.

For a right block $Ha_1,\ldots,Ha_b$, choose an injection $\mu:G/H\to\Q$ and
put
\[
  R_H(x,y)=\prod_{j=1}^b
  \bigl(\mu(xH)-\mu(ya_j^{-1}H)\bigr).
\]
Here
\[
  x^{-1}y\in Ha_j
  \Longleftrightarrow x^{-1}ya_j^{-1}\in H
  \Longleftrightarrow ya_j^{-1}H=xH.
\]
Thus the zero set is again exact, and expansion in the single row variable
$u=\mu(xH)$ gives rank at most $b+1$.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Compatible-block refinement]\label{thm:grouped}
Suppose $t\ge1$ and the forbidden cosets have a compatible block partition
$\mathcal B_1,\ldots,\mathcal B_t$, where $b_j=|\mathcal B_j|$, and set
\[
  R=\prod_{j=1}^t(b_j+1).
\]
If $A=G\setminus\bigcup_iC_i$ is nonempty, then
\[
  |A|\ge\frac{|G|}{R},\qquad
  \tau_L(A)\le R,\qquad \tau_R(A)\le R.
\]
If $|A|=|G|/R$, then $A$ is a subgroup coset of index $R$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Take one matrix from Lemma~\ref{lem:block} for each block and form their Hadamard
product $B$.  Its exact support is
$B(x,y)\ne0\Longleftrightarrow x^{-1}y\in A$, and
$\rank B\le R$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:hadamard-rank}.  Choose $z\in A$.  The entries
$B(x,xz)$ form a nonzero permutation transversal, and each row has exactly
$|A|$ nonzeros.  Hence Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse} gives
$|G|\le |A|\rank B\le |A|R$.  Its row- and column-support conclusions give
the two translate-cover bounds as in \cref{thm:translate}.

If equality holds, then $\rank B=R=|G|/|A|$.  The proof of
\cref{thm:equality}, using the supports $xA$, applies verbatim after translating
one element of $A$ to the identity.  Thus $A$ is a subgroup coset of index $R$.
\end{proof}

Singleton blocks recover $R=2^n$, while a block of size $b\ge2$ replaces
$2^b$ by the smaller factor $b+1$.  Repeated identical cosets may first be
deleted.  One may minimize $R$ over all compatible block partitions of the
given description.

\section{Sharpness and equality examples}

The grouped denominator is attained by the standard coordinate-fiber
construction, familiar from finite-grid and abelian coset-cover arguments
\cite{AlonFuredi1993,Szegedy2007,LettlSun2008,SambaleTarnauceanu2022}.

\begin{proposition}[Coordinate-fiber sharpness]\label{prop:sharp}
For every tuple of positive integers $(b_1,\ldots,b_t)$ and every finite group
$K$, the denominator $R=\prod_j(b_j+1)$ in \cref{thm:grouped} is attained.
In particular, the constant $2^{-n}$ in \cref{thm:main} is sharp.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take
\[
  G=K\times\prod_{j=1}^t C_{b_j+1}.
\]
For coordinate $j$, use the $b_j$ nonzero fibers, each a coset of the
coordinate-zero subgroup.  Their union misses exactly
$K\times\{(0,\ldots,0)\}$, which has relative size $1/R$.  Setting every
$b_j=1$ gives $G=K\times(C_2)^n$ and the sharp constant $2^{-n}$.
\end{proof}

The next transparent equality family is the index-two specialization of Sun's
stronger exact-cover construction \cite[Example~1.2, p.~417]{Sun2001}; see also \cite{Sun2006}.  It is
recorded only to contrast disjoint and overlapping equality mechanisms.

\begin{proposition}[Sun's subgroup-chain example, index-two case]\label{prop:sun-chain}
Suppose
\[
  G=H_0>H_1>\cdots>H_n,\qquad [H_{j-1}:H_j]=2,
\]
and choose $a_j\in H_{j-1}\setminus H_j$.  Then
\[
  G\setminus H_n=\bigsqcup_{j=1}^n a_jH_j.
\]
Thus these $n$ cosets attain equality in \cref{thm:main}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For each $j$, the two left cosets of $H_j$ in $H_{j-1}$ are $H_j$ and
$a_jH_j$, so $H_{j-1}\setminus H_j=a_jH_j$.  The successive differences are
disjoint and telescope to $G\setminus H_n$; multiplying the indices gives
$[G:H_n]=2^n$.
\end{proof}

The coordinate construction has substantial overlap among the forbidden
cosets, whereas the subgroup-chain layers are disjoint.  This already rules
out any equality-family classification based only on one of those two patterns.

\appendix
\section{Compact consequences}

\subsection{An abstract rank-support formulation}
For convenience we combine the two standard rank facts above in one form.

\begin{theorem}[Rank-support incidence principle]\label{thm:abstract}
Let $X$ and $Y$ be finite sets of the same positive size $N$, let $s\ge1$, and
for $1\le i\le s$ let $B_i$ be an $X\times Y$ matrix over a field $\F$ with
$\rank B_i\le r_i$.  Set $B=B_1\Had\cdots\Had B_s$.  Suppose every row of
$B$ has at most $m$ nonzeros and there is a bijection $\pi:X\to Y$ with
$B(x,\pi(x))\ne0$ for all $x$.  Then
\[
  N\le m\rank B\le m\prod_{i=1}^s r_i.
\]
At most $\prod_i r_i$ actual row supports cover $Y$, and at most
$\prod_i r_i$ actual column supports cover $X$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemmas~\ref{lem:hadamard-rank} and~\ref{lem:sparse}.  The transversal forces $m\ge1$
and $\rank B>0$, so no empty-product convention is involved.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Cayley exact-support rank}
Exact zero--nonzero pattern rank was studied before the present setting
\cite{HershkowitzSchneider1993}.  De Wolf's nondeterministic rank minimizes
complex rank over matrices nonzero exactly on the $1$-inputs of a Boolean
function \cite{DeWolf2000}; the following is its Cayley-support specialization,
with an arbitrary ground field.  Unlike graph or Haemers minrank conventions, every position allowed by the
support relation is required to be nonzero; this distinction is discussed in
\cite{DeaettYoung2026}.

\begin{definition}\label{def:mr}
For a nonempty subset $A$ of a finite group $G$ and a field $\F$, define
\[
 \mr_{\F}(A)=\min\{\rank B:
 B(x,y)\ne0\Longleftrightarrow x^{-1}y\in A\},
\]
where rows and columns are indexed by $G$.
\end{definition}

Row and column permutations show that
$\mr_{\F}(sA)=\mr_{\F}(A)=\mr_{\F}(At)$ for all $s,t\in G$.

\begin{theorem}[Exact-support bounds and equality]\label{thm:mr}
Let $A$ be a nonempty subset of a finite group $G$.
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\alph*)]
\item For every field $\F$, $\mr_{\F}(A)\ge |G|/|A|$.
\item Equality holds if and only if $A$ is a subgroup coset.
\item If $G\setminus A$ is a union of $n$ left or right subgroup cosets, then
      $\mr_{\Q}(A)\le2^n$.  A compatible block partition with denominator
      $R$ gives $\mr_{\Q}(A)\le R$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Choose an exact-support matrix $B$ of rank $\mr_{\F}(A)$.  For any
$a\in A$, it has the nonzero transversal $(x,xa)$ and exactly $|A|$
nonzeros in every row, proving (a) by Lemma~\ref{lem:sparse}.  If equality holds,
every actual row basis of $B$ has
$|G|/|A|$ supports of size $|A|$ covering $G$, so those supports are disjoint.
The disjoint-or-equal and stabilizer argument in \cref{thm:equality} then shows,
after translating one element of $A$ to the identity, that $A$ is a subgroup
coset.  Conversely, if $A=aH$, the $0$--$1$ exact-support matrix has as its
distinct rows the indicators of the left cosets of $H$; those disjoint nonzero
rows have rank $[G:H]=|G|/|A|$ over every field.  Part (c) is supplied by the
detectors in \cref{thm:main,thm:grouped}.
\end{proof}

Part (a) is the elementary sparse-transversal bound.  No historical novelty is
claimed for the equality characterization in (b); its priority in the full
arbitrary-completion setting remains unresolved.  The upper bounds in (c) are
detector-derived applications not located in the targeted search.  Related
support--dimension inequalities and subgroup-coset equality phenomena for
orbit-generated or convolutional matrices appear in
\cite{GoldsteinGuralnickIsaacs2005,BorelloWillemsZini2022,ChenFanLuo2025}, but
those results do not automatically apply to an arbitrary exact-support
completion.

For any nonempty $A\subseteq G$, let $\kappa_G(A)$ be the minimum number of
left or right subgroup cosets whose union is $G\setminus A$, with the
empty-family convention $\kappa_G(G)=0$.  If $A=G$, then
$\mr_{\Q}(G)=1$ and $\kappa_G(G)=0$.  Otherwise
\cref{thm:mr}(c) gives the immediate obstruction
\[
  \kappa_G(A)\ge\left\lceil\log_2\mr_{\Q}(A)\right\rceil.
\]

\subsection{Prescribed images and derangements}
Let a finite group $G$ act on a finite set $\Omega$, and fix pairs
$(p_i,q_i)\in\Omega^2$ for $1\le i\le n$.  For $n=0$ the avoiding set is
$G$ and all conclusions below are immediate, so assume $n\ge1$.  Each nonempty fiber
$\{g:gp_i=q_i\}$ is a left coset of the stabilizer $G_{p_i}$.
Consequently the set
\[
  D=\{g\in G:gp_i\ne q_i\text{ for every }i\}
\]
is empty or satisfies
\[
  |D|\ge |G|/2^n,\qquad \tau_L(D),\tau_R(D)\le2^n.
\]
If the nonempty constraints are grouped by source point and source $p_j$ has
$b_j$ distinct reachable forbidden images, then $2^n$ may be replaced by
$\prod_j(b_j+1)$.  These are direct point-stabilizer applications, not
independent permutation-group methods.

For derangements, put
$\Der(G,\Omega)=\{g:gp\ne p\text{ for every }p\in\Omega\}$.  If
$\Omega=\varnothing$, then $\Der(G,\Omega)=G$ directly and the bound and
one-translate covers hold with $2^{|\Omega|}=1$.  If $\Omega\ne\varnothing$,
the preceding result gives either an empty derangement set or
\[
  |\Der(G,\Omega)|\ge |G|/2^{|\Omega|},\qquad
  \tau_L(\Der),\tau_R(\Der)\le2^{|\Omega|}.
\]
For a nontrivial transitive action of degree $d$ and permutation rank $r$,
Cameron and Cohen proved the substantially stronger estimate
\[
  \frac{|\Der(G,\Omega)|}{|G|}\ge\frac{r-1}{d}\ge\frac1d,
\]
which is strictly stronger than $2^{-d}$ for $d\ge2$
\cite[p.~136]{CameronCohen1992}; see also later refinements in
\cite{BarbieriSpiga2024}.  The present statement is recorded only for
its uniform prescribed-image formulation.

\subsection{Finite-index and profinite transfers}
The common-normal-core mechanism for finite unions of mixed left and right
finite-index cosets is standard; compare the classical coset-cover framework
of \cite{Neumann1954} and the explicit common-core treatment in Button
\cite[Propositions~2.4--2.6]{Button2015}.

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:finite-index}
Let $G$ be any group, let $n\ge1$, and let $C_1,\ldots,C_n$ be left or right cosets of
finite-index subgroups.  Suppose
$A=G\setminus\bigcup_iC_i$ is nonempty.  There is a finite-index normal
subgroup $N\triangleleft G$ such that $A$ is a union of $N$-cosets and
\[
  \frac{|A/N|}{|G/N|}\ge2^{-n},
\]
where $A/N$ denotes the image of $A$ in $G/N$.
Moreover $G=T_LA=AT_R$ for finite sets $T_L,T_R$ with
$|T_L|,|T_R|\le2^n$.  A compatible block denominator $R$ replaces $2^n$ when
available.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Let $H_i$ be the subgroup underlying $C_i$ and set
$N=\bigcap_i\core_G(H_i)$, where
$\core_G(H_i)=\bigcap_{g\in G}gH_ig^{-1}$.  Each core, and hence $N$, is
finite-index normal.  Since $N\le H_i$, every $C_i$ and $A$ are full inverse
images of sets in the finite quotient $G/N$, with the original left or right
orientation retained.  Apply \cref{thm:main,thm:translate,thm:grouped} in the
quotient and lift representatives of the finite left and right translate
covers.
\end{proof}

If $G$ is profinite and the $C_i$ are cosets of open subgroups, the same $N$ is
open normal.  The complement is clopen and descends to the finite quotient;
normalized Haar measure is the quotient proportion, a standard profinite fact
\cite[pp.~24--25]{Segal2007}.  Therefore a nonempty complement satisfies
$\mu(A)\ge2^{-n}$ and has left and right translate-cover number at most $2^n$,
with the compatible-block denominator $R$ available as before.  These infinite
and profinite statements are routine transfers of the finite constants.

\section{Computation and open questions}

The proof is independent of computation.  The accompanying exact verifier uses
explicit multiplication tables and rational Gaussian elimination.  Its fresh
replay exhausts all families of at most three distinct proper coset sets in
$C_6$, $S_3$, $D_8$, $Q_8$, and $(C_2)^3$; checks every equality complement in
that census; verifies a mixed left/right $D_8$ detector and a nonnormal right
block; and checks grouped sharpness for profiles $(1,1,1)$ and $(2,3)$.  The
replay examines $29{,}505$ families, including $28{,}773$ proper unions and
$3{,}070$ equality cases, and ends with all assertions passing.  These finite
checks are regression tests, not a proof.

\begin{question}[Equality families]
Classify all equality-achieving families of $n$ left or right subgroup cosets.
Equivalently, once \cref{thm:equality} identifies the complement as a subgroup
coset of index $2^n$, classify all $n$-coset representations of its complement.
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Stability]
If $|A|$ is only slightly larger than $|G|/2^n$, under what hypotheses must $A$
be close to a subgroup coset?  Can a quantitative conclusion be extracted from
near-equality in the support-cover rank argument?
\end{question}

\begin{question}[Further compression]
Can compatible blocks, subgroup chains, repeated quotient coordinates, and
other algebraic dependencies be unified by a canonical exact-support rank
parameter sharper than $\prod_j(b_j+1)$?
\end{question}
